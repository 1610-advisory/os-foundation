# Inn rail, outside help maybe

Wednesday 8 April. Harbor Mill. The rail for Fen Coast Inn is the long job. The floor can build it. Rubbing out the oil may not fit beside the school crates.

- Lowood Lumber's sticker on the oak still says the March lot, and the count on the sticker does not match the rack.
- Register close is the usual close. A drawer off by more than $20 gets a slip, not a forced number.
- On a dry day the kiln stays off. The oil wants the slow cure.
- The bakery asked for a board count and has not sent sizes. No quote until the sizes exist.
- Sixteen hinges are on the shelf. They are not to be borrowed for the crate order.
- The loading door sticks when the wind is from the west. Lift and then pull.
- Wet doors do not get stacked. The stickers leave a mark that the oil will not hide.
- Driver copies the card onto the clipboard. The office copy stays on the nail.

This is a vendor decision, if it becomes a decision at all. No named person. No offer. No role filled.

Hire a subcontractor for the job. That sentence is the whole idea, and it is not done.

Bramble Coatings sent a one-page bid for the rub-down only. $2,400. Two days in our finish room or one day at their shop. The bid is a vendor price Harbor Mill would pay. They are a coatings shop, not a new employee. No one at Bramble is being brought onto our clock.

Open questions, half written:
- their shop or our room
- who moves the rail. our driver either way
- oil lot. ours, so the color matches the sample
- date. after the nail test, not before
- insurance paper. ask if we say yes
- do not say yes today
